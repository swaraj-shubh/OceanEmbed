    Seeing 1,000 m below the surface — from space.

    Satellites only see the top of the ocean. OTER (Ocean Thermal Embedding Reconstruction) learns what that surface is telling us about the water beneath it, and rebuilds the temperature at 15 depths, every day, over the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal.

    7 satellite maps in 15 depths out · 0–1000 m 0.25° (~28 km) · daily checked against real Argo floats
    Dive in

    The problem

    Two ways to look at the ocean. Neither is enough alone.

    Satellites

    See everywhere, every day — but only the thin surface layer.

    16,896grid cells observed every single day
    OTER learns the link between the two

    Argo floats

    Measure all the way down — but they are few and far between.

    —deep profiles in the whole region, within 5 days of 4 Dec 2023

    So we train a model on years of surface maps paired with full-depth temperatures, then ask it to fill in the ocean below — everywhere, every day. And we grade it on the real floats, which it never saw.

    3Model architecture

    Read · remember · paint

    Three parts, each with one job. Scroll through them — the diagram follows.

    ① CNN encoder — read

    Turns each day's maps into patterns

    The same convolutional network reads each of the 7 days. Every layer halves the map and doubles the detail it describes: 96×176 cells become 12×22, while 7 variables become 32 → 64 → 128 → 256 features — things like fronts, eddies and upwelling, not raw numbers.

    ② ConvLSTM — remember

    Watches how the week unfolds

    A recurrent memory steps through the 7 days in order, keeping what matters and forgetting what doesn't. Its final state — 256 × 12 × 22 numbers — is the OceanEmbed: a compact fingerprint of the ocean's recent history.

    ③ U-Net decoder — paint

    Paints the temperature back at full size

    The decoder upsamples the fingerprint back to 96×176. Skip connections hand over the sharp details the encoder saw on the last day, so coastlines and fronts stay crisp.

    ④ Output — 15 depths at once

    A full 3-D ocean, every day

    4Training · target GLORYS12V1

    Learning from a teacher

    GLORYS12V1 is a global ocean reanalysis — a physics model fed with every available observation — with full temperature profiles everywhere. The model learns to reproduce it at 15 depths from 0 to 1000 m, from surface data alone.

    Split by time, never shuffled

    The model is always judged on a future it has never seen.

    Masked loss

    Land, missing cells and depths below the seafloor are masked out — the model is never rewarded or punished there.

    7Independent evaluation · Argo

    The exam it never studied for

    Argo floats are robots that sink to the deep, drift, then rise to the surface measuring temperature all the way up. None of them were used for training — so they are a fair, independent test.

    Match

    Same 0.25° grid cell, within ±1 day, at valid depths.

    Interpolate

    Resample the float's hundreds of readings onto our 15 depths (valid depths only).

    Compare

    Model vs float, at every matched location and depth.

    Score

    RMSE, MAE, bias and correlation — reported depth by depth.

    Primary benchmark: raw Argo profiles (2023–2024). Secondary: INCOIS gridded Argo (1° / 10-day), compared after averaging our output up to its coarser grid.
